Where I have been

Context

  • Subject and range: Computer Science across Years 7 to 12, from KS3 foundations through to the KS5 OCR H446 specification.
  • Setting: The Garibaldi School, including KS5 transition work towards Melton Vale (MV16).
  • The shift this year: from enthusiastic, content-rich delivery to deliberate, evidence-led teaching, shaped by observation feedback and live pupil data.
How to read this deck: Part One covers Standards 1 to 8, one slide each, and Part Two covers professional conduct. Each slide shows where I started, what I changed, and the impact, with three pieces of evidence.
Part One - Standard 1

Set High Expectations

Set high expectations which inspire, motivate and challenge pupils.
  • Started: I leant on rapport and enthusiasm, and learnt that high expectations have to be explicit and visible, not assumed.
  • Changed: silent starts with screens locked, clear "do now" code-debugging routines, and genuine stretch tasks for high attainers.
  • Beyond the timetable: I run a 3D Animation (Blender) club and a Morning Hub, so quieter pupils now arrive early to code.
Evidence
Part One - Standard 2

Promote Good Progress

Promote good progress and outcomes by pupils. (Overlaps with the Learning Process and Assessment strands.)
  • Started: I talked about pupils making progress without much hard evidence behind it.
  • Changed: every pupil response is captured and marked in Classfolio. I moderate the marks, then set targets directly from the data.
  • Impact: 26 moderated assessment runs across 20 classes, plus nine Year 12 mocks marked against the mark scheme with the class teacher.

Part One - Standard 3

Subject and Curriculum Knowledge

Demonstrate good subject and curriculum knowledge.
  • KS5 confidence: self-studied the OCR H446 specification and object-oriented programming in Python to deliver sixth-form content securely.
  • Bespoke resources: designed interactive, self-coded webpages to teach hard concepts such as pathfinding algorithms.
  • Subject CPD: completed STEM Learning modules on Von Neumann architecture, networks and cyber security.
Evidence
Part One - Standard 4

Plan and Teach Well-Structured Lessons

Plan and teach well structured lessons.
  • Structured for learning: moved to PRIMM and Rosenshine small steps with clear modelling, "I do, We do, You do".
  • Time used well: planned deliberate transitions and checkpoints so lesson time is not lost.
  • Reflective: acted on observations of my modelling to sharpen explanations of new and unfamiliar content.
Evidence
Part One - Standard 5

Adapt Teaching

Adapt teaching to respond to the strengths and needs of all pupils.
  • Planned, not improvised: I keep a per-class inclusive planning grid mapping each pupil's SEND or EAL need to specific strategies.
  • EAL access: built a bilingual Farsi and English interactive assessment so a new-arrival pupil could access the same content.
  • Scaffold, never water down: rebuilt a lesson structurally for a lower band rather than reducing the content.
Evidence
Part One - Standard 6

Make Accurate Use of Assessment

Make accurate and productive use of assessment.
  • The 80% rule: a hard gate on live Classfolio data. If fewer than 80% answer correctly, I reteach rather than move on.
  • Surfacing gaps: hinge questions and targeted cold-calling to find misconceptions early, not at the end.
  • Low-stakes retrieval: gamified plenaries to check what has actually stuck.
Evidence
Part One - Standard 7

Manage Behaviour

Manage behaviour effectively to ensure a good and safe learning environment.
  • My honest development area: observers repeatedly flagged that I relied on rapport and applied consequences too late.
  • What I changed: built clear routines, visual timers and firm cut-offs, and now use the C1 and C2 system consistently and early.
  • The lesson: consistent consequences protect positive relationships rather than damage them.
Evidence
Part One - Standard 8

Wider Professional Responsibilities

Fulfil wider professional responsibilities.
  • Parent communication: trusted with the majority of Year 7 and Year 8 appointments, I built a data lookup tool to give parents precise, evidence-based feedback.
  • Collaboration: built department-wide Canva and PhotoPea resources with my mentor.
  • Professional engagement: engaged with KS5 transition at Melton Vale and contributed to PSHE and Unifrog.
Evidence
Part Two

Personal and Professional Conduct

Uphold public trust and high standards of ethics, safeguarding and professional conduct.
  • Safeguarding: completed statutory training including Prevent (Channel, Referrals and Awareness), FGM, and Health and Safety.
  • Reflective practice: logged professional development consistently across every DLD cycle, responding to feedback throughout.
  • British values and wellbeing: promoted these through PSHE and Unifrog, working within school policies and statutory frameworks.
Evidence
